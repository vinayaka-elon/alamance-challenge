Alamance Growth Data Challenge

Where is Alamance County growing, which factors are associated with that growth, where is growth likely to occur next, and what should local decision makers do about it?

Alamance County added roughly 35,000 residents between 2010 and 2025, and new homes, jobs and rising prices are not spread evenly across it. Your team will find where growth is happening, what goes with it and where it is likely to go next. Your work ends with three tasks.

1
Explain

Build a diagnostic model of what is associated with growth.

2
Forecast

Build a predictive model of where growth goes next, and compare it with a simple rule.

3
Recommend Required

Name who should act, what they should do, the evidence behind it and one limit of the data.

You deliver
A team presentation, plus the files that reproduce every result you show and your AI-use log.
How to work
Pick one sharp question and three to five measures that bear on it, then learn each measure well: how it was measured, where it is missing and what it cannot show.
Suggested path 1. Geography2. Data dictionary3. Questions4. Documenting AI

02Geography: from state to parcel

Every dataset in this challenge is recorded at one geographic level. Parcels sit inside towns, townships, ZIP codes and census tracts, but ZIP codes ignore county and town lines, so a ZIP figure can describe people who live outside Alamance. Step through the levels to see what each one is, which data uses it and how it connects to the others.

Parcels, tracts and townships nest cleanly inside the county. Towns and ZIP codes do not: a town can annex land across a township line, and five of the nine ZIP areas cross into neighboring counties.

03ZIP code trends over time

The ZIP panel workbook follows the nine ZIP areas that cover Alamance from 2010 to 2025. Use it to scan the 19 measures quickly and shortlist the two or three that tell the clearest story for your question. Gray ZIPs have no published value for that year.

Click a ZIP on the map or a line to highlight it. Census survey values are 5-year averages labeled by their last year.

04Data and merging

Two workbooks and one notebook support the challenge. Most analysis errors in projects like this come from joins that silently duplicate or drop rows, so plan every join and check the row count after it.

Alamance_Growth_Starter_Data.xlsx

  • Parcels: 79,870 properties, 2026 snapshot
  • Permits: 27,241 county permits, 2019 to Aug 2026
  • Areas: 10 towns plus Unincorporated

Alamance_Growth_ZIP_Panel.xlsx

  • 9 ZIP areas × 16 years, 41 measures
  • Census survey, IRS, business patterns, jobs, house prices
  • Data_Availability sheet shows coverage by year

Alamance_Growth_Notebook.ipynb

  • Loads both workbooks and checks every join
  • Assigns parcels and permits to ZIP codes
  • Builds a ZIP-year table, one explanatory model and one forecast
Try a join: why row counts change

Five permits are joined to parcels on Parcel ID. One parcel ID appears twice on the Parcels sheet, which happens 123 times in the real file.

The five checks for every join
  1. Keys as text. Store keys as text so leading zeros survive (Parcel ID, ZIP Code).
  2. Duplicates first. Resolve duplicate keys on the "one" side before joining, and write down the rule.
  3. Summarize. Summarize the "many" side first (permits per parcel, homes built per ZIP and year).
  4. Row counts. Compare row counts before and after; a left join must keep the left table's count.
  5. Unmatched rows. Count and inspect unmatched rows; about 14% of permits have no Parcel ID.

    07AI in analytics: what the research shows
    
  
    AI tools can write code, summarize data and draft reports quickly, and the research on their use in data analysis points to one consistent lesson: the analyst's job shifts toward checking. Read these findings before you start.
    
  
    
  
    
    Where this challenge sits on the AI Assessment Scale
    
    The scale describes five levels of permitted AI use (Perkins et al., 2024). This challenge is set at level 5: AI may be used throughout, and teams are encouraged to apply it in new ways, provided every use is recorded and checked.

    A check that counts
    A check qualifies when it could have revealed an error. Recomputing a number, comparing row counts, rerunning code on a case with a known answer and tracing a chart back to its cells all qualify. A general impression that the output looked right does not.
    
    
    Closing reflection, a short paragraph at the end of the log
    1. Where did AI save time or improve the work?
    2. Where was it wrong or misleading, and how did you find out?
    3. Which decisions did the team keep for itself, and why?
    
  
    
  
    
    Things to explore with AI
    
    Judges reward approaches that go beyond standard methods. These uses of AI have a research base; whichever you try, compare the AI step with the same step done without AI and log what you found.
